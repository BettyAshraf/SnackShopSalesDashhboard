# Business Insights & Recommendations

## 1. Executive Summary
The supplied sample records show **$5,621 in sales revenue** from **297 units** across 7 transaction rows between **1 and 4 January 2026**. Drinks contributed approximately **64.4%** of revenue, while Snacks contributed approximately **35.6%**. Juice was the leading product by both revenue and quantity.

These findings are descriptive of the supplied sample only. The short four-day period and small number of records do not support conclusions about seasonality, long-term demand, or sustained business performance.

## 2. Product Performance

| Product | Quantity Sold | Sales Revenue | Revenue Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Juice | 107 | $2,461 | 43.8% |
| Chips | 50 | $1,000 | 17.8% |
| Water | 60 | $660 | 11.7% |
| Biscuits | 40 | $520 | 9.3% |
| Soda | 20 | $500 | 8.9% |
| Chocolate | 20 | $480 | 8.5% |
| **Total** | **297** | **$5,621** | **100%** |

### Insight 1 — Juice leads sales
Juice generated **$2,461**, the highest revenue of all products, and accounted for approximately **43.8%** of total revenue. It also recorded the highest sales volume at **107 units**.

**Recommendation:** Keep Juice easy to find and monitor its availability. If this pattern continues over a longer period, consider testing a small promotion or a complementary-product offer. Avoid increasing stock solely on the basis of this short sample.

### Insight 2 — Chips is the second-largest revenue contributor
Chips generated **$1,000**, approximately **17.8%** of revenue, from 50 units.

**Recommendation:** Track Chips alongside Juice when reviewing product performance. A bundle test involving Juice and Chips could be evaluated, with results measured using additional transaction data.

### Insight 3 — Water has meaningful unit volume
Water sold **60 units**, the second-highest quantity, and generated **$660** in revenue.

**Recommendation:** Monitor Water availability because it has relatively high unit volume. Evaluate its performance over a longer period before changing its shelf space or replenishment level.

### Insight 4 — Lower-revenue products need more context
Soda and Chocolate each sold 20 units. Their revenues were $500 and $480 respectively. These figures alone do not show whether either product is profitable or underperforming because product costs and stock availability are not provided.

**Recommendation:** Compare these products using a longer sales history, unit costs, gross profit, and stockout information before making assortment decisions.

## 3. Category Performance

| Category | Sales Revenue | Revenue Share | Units Sold |

| Drinks | $3,621 | 64.4% | 197 |
| Snacks | $2,000 | 35.6% | 100 |

### Insight 5 — Drinks contribute the larger share
Drinks generated **$3,621**, approximately **64.4%** of total revenue, and 197 units. Snacks generated **$2,000**, approximately **35.6%**, and 100 units.

**Recommendation:** Review the Drinks category's availability and product mix, while continuing to monitor Snacks. The category split is a revenue comparison, not a profit comparison.

## 4. Recommended Next Steps
1. Collect several weeks or months of transactions to identify reliable demand patterns.
2. Add product cost and operating expense data to enable gross-profit and margin analysis.
3. Add stock-on-hand and stockout data to distinguish low demand from unavailable products.
4. Track average selling price, daily revenue, and units sold over time.
5. Validate dashboard slicers so that selecting a product or category filters every relevant KPI, chart, and table.
6. Revisit product and category recommendations after a larger dataset is available.

## 5. Data Limitations
- Only 7 transaction rows are available.
- The data covers four calendar days (1–4 January 2026).
- There are no cost or expense fields, so profit and profit margin cannot be calculated.
- There is no inventory or stockout information.
- Revenue is calculated as quantity multiplied by unit price; it does not account for discounts, taxes, returns, or other adjustments because those fields are not present.

**Conclusion:** In this sample, Juice is the strongest product by revenue and units sold, and Drinks is the leading category by revenue. These are useful descriptive findings, but they should be validated with a larger, longer, and more complete dataset before operational decisions are made.
